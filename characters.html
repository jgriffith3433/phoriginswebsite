Who sits at the table

Chairman, Hawkes Biomedical

Pierce Hawkes

There’s always room for grace.

He sits at the head of the table because he built the table. To Pierce, market share is the language of people who have already lost. He keeps the board hungry, the street impressed, and B3 dark by decree, and he calls that leadership.

When his own terminal puts a shape on the 58th floor that should not have one, he does not send a team. He takes the elevator. The man who walks into the vat room is sure the ceiling is his. The man who walks out is less sure he is still that man.

Director

Hale

If that basement leaks, the valuation does not survive the quarter.

Hale watches the street so the chairman does not have to. She is the one who says a secret lab is not a science problem. It is a number, and numbers die in public. She did not come up fifty-eight floors to admire the skyline.

The charter

Voss

Those are patients down there, Pierce. Not prototypes.

Voss still uses the words the charter was written in. Ethics. Seal it. Patients. He is the last person at the table treating B3 as a ward instead of a pipeline. Pierce has already finished that argument. Voss has not.

The timeline

Lang

Investors want a date they can take to Zurich.

Lang keeps the outside world in the room: the calendar, the promise, the people who will not wait while a chairman moves the goal. Calm about it. That is the job. Zurich does not want a sermon. It wants a date.

After B3

What walks out

I can still think. That is not the same as still being me.

The basement does not end the company. It ends the certainty. The vat breaks, and the acid writes the chairman into something that still answers to his name. He is the first to hear the difference.
